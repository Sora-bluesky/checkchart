# Principles for readable diagrams

Read a source for facts, then choose a diagram for one reader and one question. A copied layout is not evidence that the resulting diagram answers that question. Structural rules fail the check; numeric limits only advise the author.

## Principles and public sources

| Principle | Evidence and qualification | Application in checkchart |
| --- | --- | --- |
| State the reader, question, kind and scope before drawing. | The [C4 model checklist](https://c4model.com/diagrams/checklist) and [notation guidance](https://c4model.com/diagrams/notation) ask for an audience, title, type and scope. [Architecture diagram guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/well-architected/architect-role/design-diagrams) recommends a purpose and audience for each view. | `PurposeStated` requires non-empty `title`, `reader`, `question`. Reader and question are report metadata. |
| Keep one abstraction level and one or two concerns per chart. | [C4 model diagrams](https://c4model.com/diagrams) separate levels. The critiques of [diagram mistakes](https://www.ilograph.com/blog/posts/diagram-mistakes/) and [further mistakes](https://www.ilograph.com/blog/posts/more-common-diagram-mistakes/) discuss mixed levels and overloaded master diagrams. | Optional architecture `level`; `MixedLevels` advises separate charts. Split scope before copying more facts. |
| Use containment only when elements actually exist or run within the boundary. Keep context shallow. | [Diagram-mistakes guidance](https://www.ilograph.com/blog/posts/diagram-mistakes/) discusses misleading nesting and missing context; [architecture guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/well-architected/architect-role/design-diagrams) distinguishes actual containment. | `DeepNesting` warns above two zone levels. Zones with `untrusted: true` use dashed borders and pass that classification to all nested components. Plain groups do not add a trust restriction. `TrustChecked` requires the trusted receiver to check incoming untrusted data, including the incoming direction of a `both` connection. A zone's label should say what boundary it represents. |
| Remove an outer container that groups only one inner container. | [Palmer and Rock (1994)](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/BF03200760.pdf) study connectedness as a grouping cue. The redundant-container rule is a design inference from grouping principles, not a directly measured effect. | `NoRedundantContainer` rejects a zone with exactly one child zone and no direct component. Merge meanings into the inner label, or show the intended sibling. Single-participant sequence groups remain allowed. |
| Label each arrow with intent, direction and exchanged information. | The [C4 model checklist](https://c4model.com/diagrams/checklist) asks for labeled, unidirectional relationships. [Architecture guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/well-architected/architect-role/design-diagrams) and [diagram critiques](https://www.ilograph.com/blog/posts/diagram-mistakes/) reject ambiguous relationships. | `ArrowsLabeled` rejects unlabeled drawn architecture connections. `GenericLabel` warns about labels such as "uses" or "通信" without an object. |
| Show request and response separately, or explain each direction. | [Architecture diagram guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/well-architected/architect-role/design-diagrams) and [C4 model notation](https://c4model.com/diagrams/notation) favor a clear direction and relationship meaning. | `TwoWayArrow` advises two one-way arrows or an explicit label for both directions. |
| Use a sequence diagram when repeated exchanges dominate a flow. | The conveyor-belt critique in [further diagram mistakes](https://www.ilograph.com/blog/posts/more-common-diagram-mistakes/) warns that a linear flow can hide interactions and conditions. | `FlowLoopsBack` advises a sequence diagram above two backward edges in declared node order. Two is checkchart's choice. |
| Reduce crossings; keep the path being followed continuous and straight. | [Purchase (1997)](https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/which-aesthetic-has-the-greatest-effect-on-human-understanding/) found crossings important in the studied tasks; generalization is limited by the experimental sample. [Ware et al. (2002)](https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/cognitive-measurements-of-graph-aesthetics/) examine path length and continuity as well as crossings. | ELK uses orthogonal routing. `Crossings` counts transverse intersections in the final SVG layout. Main-path edges receive high straightness and direction priorities; first decision branches define that path without guessing from ids. |
| Prefer right-angle crossings when a crossing cannot be removed. | [Huang et al. (2014), DOI 10.1016/j.jvlc.2014.03.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jvlc.2014.03.001) examine graph aesthetics and comprehension, including crossing angles. | Orthogonal connectors keep segment crossings at right angles. Rounded bends do not change the underlying routes used for counting. |
| Guide reading in one direction, with consistent sizes for the same kind of element. | [Ambler's diagram style guidance](https://agilemodeling.com/style/general.htm), [flowcharting guidance](https://info.aiim.org/aiim-blog/flowcharting-in-business-process-management) and [Lucidchart's flowchart guidance](https://help.lucid.co/hc/en-us/articles/16300314888724-Create-a-flowchart-in-Lucidchart) recommend consistent direction and appearance. | Model direction controls layout. Same-kind shapes use the chart's maximum measured size. Use verb plus noun for flow steps and name plus role for components. |
| Keep groups and parallel tasks small enough to follow. | [Cowan (2001)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11515286/) argues for about four chunks in short-term memory under particular conditions. [Ambler's activity-diagram guidance](https://agilemodeling.com/style/activityDiagram.htm) recommends fewer than five swimlanes. Neither measured checkchart's zone, branch or total-node limits. | Warnings above five direct zone children or parallel branches and above twelve nodes/components are checkchart's choices, not research-derived pass/fail limits. |
| Limit color categories and give every category a stable meaning. | [Moody (2009)](https://research.utwente.nl/en/publications/the-physics-of-notations-a-scientific-approach-to-designing-visua/) discusses semiotic clarity and visual variables. The approximately six-category value comes through a [secondary source](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/198951), not verified primary numeric evidence. Lucidchart's guidance recommends three colors. | Warn above six used color groups. Prefer fewer when possible. Unused palette entries do not count. |
| Do not rely on color alone; keep labels close to what they explain. | Moody's principles include dual coding and visual expressiveness. [Chandler and Sweller (1992)](https://www.e-teaching.org/materialien/literatur/chandler-sweller-1992) study split attention; the diagram application is a design inference. The [C4 model checklist](https://c4model.com/diagrams/checklist) asks for comprehensible notation. | Group names appear as text in legends or bands. Arrow labels stay on their connectors. Legends explain notation; they do not replace action labels. |
| Ask a different reader whether the diagram is understandable. | The [C4 model checklist](https://c4model.com/diagrams/checklist) includes questions about elements, relationships, notation and context that structural validation cannot settle. | Hand the SVG to a fresh session or person. Ask whether every element, arrow and color is understood and whether the diagram answers its stated question. |

## Advisory limits

All thresholds live in `scripts/passes/readability.ts`. They are warnings, not universal cognitive limits or structural failures.

| Measure | Warn when | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zone depth | More than 2 levels, counting the outermost as 1 | Practice guidance on context. |
| Flow nodes or architecture components | More than 12 | checkchart's choice after Cowan's chunking argument and Lucidchart's advice to split busy charts. |
| Sequence participants | More than 6 | checkchart's choice; no study measured this limit. |
| Direct children in one zone | More than 5, counting child zones and direct components | checkchart's choice after Cowan and Ambler; not a zone experiment. |
| Branches in one `par` | More than 5, including nested blocks independently | checkchart's choice after Cowan and Ambler; not a concurrency experiment. |
| Used color groups | More than 6 | Moody via a secondary source; Lucidchart recommends 3. |
| Backward flow edges | More than 2 | checkchart's choice after the conveyor-belt critique; declared node order defines earlier. |
| Final-layout crossings | More than 0 | checkchart's choice, informed by graph-comprehension studies. Requires SVG layout; shared ends, contacts and collinear routes do not count. |
| Drawn arrows | More than about 20 | Existing checkchart choice, shared with the statechart convention. Notes and legend links do not count. |

Numeric advice can justify a split, but cannot decide the reader's task. A warning does not authorize dropping a fact needed to answer the question or explain a failure.
